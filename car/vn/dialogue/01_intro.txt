== intro
Narrator: It doesn't matter how good or bad things get - even beneath the all-seeing red eye of Jupiter, everyone's gotta make a buck.
Narrator: The boom times, when my pops moved out to the Jovian moons as a volunteer for the Giant Leap Pioneer Project, are long over.
Narrator: The bubble dreams of our fathers popped. The lost children of that era lived as gig-economy freeters, junkies, and burnouts.
Narrator: Many were gazing bleary-eyed at the end of a gunbarrel - until the invasion of Io.
Narrator: But war is every fool's market, and whenever the bull comes to ride, sex and violence are top sellers.
Narrator: There was plenty of the latter when the bugs came to Io. We even got some of the former when the lookieloos and digital nomads came.
Narrator: The XO cut radio silence and started selling licenses for "civilian war journalism" to fund the war effort and keep morale up.
Narrator: More for the Treasury Committee than for the actual soldiers and their families.
Narrator: Streamers with scanty digital avatars did the best, no matter what any scholar or activist wanted to change about society.
Narrator: Fresh meat looking to pay off mortgages and bad loans flooded the frontlines.
Narrator: Everyone back home, too scared to dip in the blood, was nevertheless peckish for realistic, raw sadist or revenge footage.
Narrator: My meal ticket came when one of these "Virtual Youtubers" wandered into the grinder armed with just her selfie stick.
Narrator: Her JustFans wasn't paying the bills, and the war was perfect for her adrenaline-junkie nerves.
Narrator: On a spit and a handshake, we became partners.
Narrator: Our contract: I keep her breathing, and she shakes her big charisma for the audience. We cut it all fifty-fifty, profit and expense.
Narrator: Death clause: all the belongings of the deceased transfer to the living member - after a Space Vultures Guild investigation confirms no foul play.
Narrator: The moonchild of this beautiful relationship was our first thousand subs.
Narrator: Donations with a message of support or snark - "hyperchats" - were now enabled. After a savage eighty percent cut for "taxes and services."
Narrator: The low-hanging fruit was running out. Soon the crackpots and the spooks were hand in hand with the grunts in one symbiotic pool of entrepreneurship.
Narrator: More and more rumblings of foul play and conspiracy started hitting the net - and making quick cash.
Narrator: That said, the simplicity of war joined with the guarantee of return finally made the present clear, if not bright:
Narrator: [i]Stay alive, and elbow out the competition with the wildest content you can get away with monetizing. Those are the rules of the War Idols.[/i]
